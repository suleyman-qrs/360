Food Cartels The Data Company That Never Sold a Pound of Meat September 15, 2026

By Kade Rourke // Trois Cent 360

Here's how a cartel runs when nobody has to shake hands in a back room anymore. You build a data company. Every competitor "voluntarily" feeds it their pricing numbers. The data company hands back a picture clean enough that nobody has to guess what the next guy's about to charge.

That's the arrangement the U.S. Department of Justice just unwound in a settlement with Agri Stats, an agricultural data firm accused of passing sensitive pricing and production numbers between competing meat processors — Tyson, Cargill, Sanderson Farms, Butterball, and JBS are among the companies named across the related litigation. An independent economic analysis found turkey processing margins roughly tripled over just a few years while the data-sharing was active, and fell again once Agri Stats briefly suspended its reporting programs in 2019 under legal pressure. A separate, ongoing DOJ investigation is looking at the beef industry, where four companies control most of the U.S. market, after a price jump last year triggered a presidential order to examine possible collusion.

No smoke-filled room required. Just a shared spreadsheet and an industry small enough that "competitor" is basically a formality.
